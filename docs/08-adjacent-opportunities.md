# Opportunities with the same shape as Acquired

Researched 20 Aug 2026. Desk research only — SERP sweep plus targeted page reads. No
practitioner interviews, so treat the ranking as a hypothesis about where to look next, not a
conclusion. Where a claim rests on content marketing rather than a primary source, it says so.

---

## Part 1 — The pattern, stated as a test

Acquired is not "AI for buying businesses". Structurally it is:

> An **origination process that nobody runs systematically**, feeding an **institutional gate
> that receives submissions in the wrong shape**, where the work in between is **legible
> evidence** and the gate itself **cannot be automated away**.

Seven components. A candidate opportunity has the same shape only if it scores on most of them.

| | Component | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High-value, low-frequency transaction** | Justifies software spend per event; nobody has tooling because they do it twice a decade |
| 2 | **Origination is manual and random** | The discovery half — no ranked pipeline exists, people work from registers and word of mouth |
| 3 | **A named institutional gatekeeper** | Bank, regulator, insurer, network operator, agency — one party whose yes/no decides the outcome |
| 4 | **The gate's standard is knowable but unpublished** | Real thresholds live in credit policy, assessor practice, method guidance — learnable, not documented |
| 5 | **Submissions arrive in the wrong shape** | Rework loops, stopped clocks, declines on presentation rather than merit — this is the measurable pain |
| 6 | **The evidence is legible** | Documents, financials, registers, standards — extractable and checkable, so "evidence or nothing" is achievable |
| 7 | **Full automation is unacceptable** | Trust, compliance or negotiation risk forces a human on the gate — which is a moat, not a limitation |

The eighth, commercial, test: **incumbents serve one end or the other, never the join.** If
someone already sits on the gate side with funding, the opportunity is smaller than it looks.

## Part 3 — The three worth actually considering

### 1. Family-farm succession finance — the same product, a bigger asset

This is Acquired with land instead of goodwill, and it is arguably a *better* fit than SME
acquisition because the collateral problem inverts.

- Over a quarter of Australian farms are run by owners aged 65+ (RSM).
- Average farm debt reached ~A$1.1m in 2024, roughly double a decade earlier (Bloomberg).
- Rising land values mean successors must take on more debt to buy out parents and siblings —
  the transfer itself is the financing event.
- The Regional Investment Corporation's AgriStarter loan (up to $2m) exists specifically for
  first-time farmers and succession, and demand reflects the affordability gap.

Why the shape fits: origination is entirely relationship-driven and there is no ranked pipeline
of "farms whose owner is 68 with no documented successor". The gate is an ag lender or the RIC,
with a knowable but unpublished standard. The evidence is unusually legible — land title,
production records, commodity yields, water entitlements. And the human gate is unavoidable
because these are family negotiations.

Why it might not: the buyer is usually the son or daughter, so *discovery* matters far less than
in SME acquisition. Strip out sourcing and you are left with the finance-packaging half only.
That halves the product. Worth testing whether an off-farm buyer / corporate-ag segment exists
in enough volume.

### 2. Renewable grid connection — the strongest non-obvious one

The gate here is a DNSP plus AEMO under NER clauses 5.3.4A/5.3.4B, and the pain is quantified
in a 2026 survey of 36 Australian energy-project leaders (commissioned by Intium, so read it as
vendor-sponsored but internally consistent):

- **75%** of projects did not achieve grid connection on time.
- **70%** said approval took 13 months or more; **20%** took two to three years; **20%** who
  reached commissioning were 18+ months late.
- **33%** named "initial application submission and completeness checks" as a cause of delay —
  the exact failure mode Acquired addresses.
- **44%** named changing technical requirements; **78%** blew through their network-connection
  cost modelling; **69%** missed financial close as a result.
- Grid connection is 10–20% of capex but up to ~50% of a project's risk profile.
- Asked how to de-risk: **75%** want standardised approval processes, **72%** want transparency
  on network capacity.

That last figure is the discovery half. "Where is there network headroom, near land I can
secure, that a DNSP will actually connect" is the sourcing-and-ranking problem, and developers
say the data isn't shared. So both halves are present: an unserved origination layer and a
gatekeeper drowning in incomplete applications.

Caveats worth being honest about. The engineering is deep — power-system modelling (PSCAD, load
flow) is not document extraction, and "evidence or nothing" is much harder when the evidence is
a simulation. Incumbents are engineering consultancies, who will say software can't do this and
will be partly right. And the buyer is a developer or an EPC, not a bank — a different sale.

### 3. Licensed practice rollups — the safest extension

Accounting, financial advice, dental, vet and childcare practices are in the same succession
wave, with two additions: licensing creates a second gate (transfer of an AFSL, a provider
registration, an approved-provider status), and valuations run on revenue multiples that a
buyer can only trust with client-retention evidence.

Reported context: 48% of Baby Boomer owners aged 60–78 plan to exit within one to five years,
87% citing retirement, and only ~22% of businesses with principals 60+ have a documented
succession plan (Scalesuite). Banks are tightening goodwill LVRs, producing the funding gap
that pushes deals to mezzanine or private equity (broker content — unverified).

This isn't a different product. It's Acquired with a vertical-specific ripeness model and a
second compliance gate. Its value is as a *second design partner track*, not a second company.

## Part 4 — The ones to walk past, and why

**Commercial insurance placement.** The pattern fits almost perfectly — underwriters name
exactly the Acquired failure modes: incomplete proposals, "quick emails with very basic
information and not much else to work with", poorly structured submissions, missing loss runs,
non-compliance with carrier formats, and for complex risks three or four back-and-forth emails
per submission. But the gate side is already being built out in 2026: Cytora shipped Autopilot,
Convr sells an underwriting workbench, and in Australia Hutch Underwriting launched **Dawn**,
which takes broker submissions in any format and returns a quoted premium into Ebix Sunrise.
The join is closing. This is the clearest illustration of the eighth test failing.

**R&D tax incentive claims.** The gate pain is real and expensive — claims are rejected for lack
of contemporaneous evidence, with one documented case losing 93% of a $1.3m claim and repaying
$205k plus $51k penalties and $75k interest. "Evidence or nothing" is literally the compliance
standard. But there is no origination gap: clients come to you, and the incumbent consultants
take a success fee for exactly this work. Gate-half only.

**Council DA / planning.** Strong gate mechanics — an RFI pauses the assessment clock and is
cited as the single biggest cause of delay, with NSW DAs averaging ~80 days against a 50-day
target, and councils now rejecting portal lodgements outright as incomplete. But it's a
compliance product, not a financing one, and the discovery side is partly served by zoning and
DA-lead tools. Real business, different company.

**Government tenders.** 40–80 hours per bid response and one missing document means
disqualification. But tender-alert services already do discovery and AutogenAI-class tools
already do the pack. Taken.

**Equipment / asset finance.** Low-doc submissions (ABN, ID, bank statements) already return
decisions in ~24 hours. The rework pain that makes Acquired valuable has largely been
engineered out. Avoid.

---

## Part 5 — What this says about Acquired

The reusable asset isn't the SME-acquisition domain model. It's the spine:

1. A ranked, evidence-cited origination layer over messy public and vendor data.
2. Provenance as a field on every derived value, not a log line.
3. Human gates as first-class objects — draft, review, release, audit.
4. A pack generator that emits from structured data, so the gate's format can change without a
   rewrite.

Every candidate above needs all four. That's an argument for building D1 and D6 as a
domain-agnostic pipeline with SME acquisition as the first configuration — but only if it costs
nothing extra now. Do not let it justify abstraction on day one; M0 is due 28 Aug.

The one finding to act on immediately: **the insurance case shows how fast the join can close.**
The lender leg being empty today is a timing advantage, not a structural one.
