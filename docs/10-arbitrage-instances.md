# Where the completeness arbitrage exists — a wider sweep

20 Aug 2026. Extends [09-completeness-arbitrage.md](09-completeness-arbitrage.md), which defined
the mechanism. This one hunts instances. Organised by **failure mechanic**, not by industry,
because the mechanic is what transfers — once you can name the five mechanics you can find new
instances yourself without another research pass.

Desk research. Vendor-sourced figures flagged inline; several vendors sell into the exact pain
they measure, which inflates numbers in a predictable direction.

---

## The finding that changes the thesis

**Revised 20 Aug 2026 after primary-source verification — see
[11-verified-round-1.md](11-verified-round-1.md). The original version of this section
overstated the case. The corrected version is stronger.**

The ICC Banking Commission's Technical Advisory Briefing No. 3 (27 June 2022) opens with this,
verbatim:

> "It is estimated that the global percentage of documents refused on first presentation under
> documentary credits ranges between 65-80%."

That is now traced to a primary ICC document rather than repeated vendor folklore. Note it is an
ICC *estimate*, not a measured statistic — worth saying when quoting it.

The briefing's own diagnosis is the useful part:

> "the primary problem lies not with the UCP 600, but with how the rules are used, i.e., through
> application and practice."

And the ICC's 2017 Trade Survey found the refusal rate had barely moved: 26.7% of respondents saw
a decrease, 12.3% an increase, **58.9% no change at all.** Decades of a published rulebook, no
improvement.

But the original claim here — "publishing the checklist doesn't fix completeness" — is too broad.
The FDA case disconfirms it for one of two distinct layers, and separating them is the real
finding:

**Layer 1 — clerical completeness.** Is the form filled in, are the attachments attached, do the
cross-references resolve? **Structured submission does fix this.** FDA guidance states that
eSTAR submissions "are not anticipated to undergo a refuse to accept (RTA) process" precisely
because a properly prepared template "should represent a complete submission" — replaced by a
technical screen. RTA hold rates fell from ~60% in the early programme to ~33%. The gate
engineered the clerical problem out at the form layer. **Anyone whose product is form-filling is
building on ground the gatekeeper can take away.**

**Layer 2 — substantive completeness.** Is the *evidence* sufficient and persuasive against a
tacit standard? Structure does not touch this. Even under mandatory eSTAR, roughly **67% of
510(k) submissions still receive at least one Additional Information request in the first review
cycle** — and the review clock pauses while you answer. No template can encode "this add-back
will be contested" or "this predicate won't persuade the reviewer".

So the defensible product isn't "we know the secret list" and isn't "we fill the form". It is
**evidence sufficiency** — anticipating what the assessor will find inadequate and pre-empting
it. That is the layer that survives the gate becoming transparent, because it requires judgement
about a standard that cannot be reduced to required fields.

Two qualifications worth keeping honest. UCP 600 and ISBP 745 are *paid ICC publications*, and
the ICC itself lists "restricted access to ISBP 745 and/or ICC opinions" among the causes of
discrepancies — so "published" is doing less work in my original argument than I implied.
And the ICC's own remedy list is entirely education, better credit drafting, and advising
beneficiaries to buy a copy of ISBP 745. Nothing structural. Which is itself the point.

## The five mechanics

### Mechanic A — The statutory clock-stop
*Incompleteness pauses or resets a legal timer. The penalty is calendar time you cannot buy back.*

**ACCC mandatory merger notification (from 1 Jan 2026) — the freshest gate in the country.**
Australia's new mandatory merger regime has an explicit power to declare a notification
**"materially incomplete"** at any point in Phase 1, plus stop-the-clock on information requests.
The ACCC clears ~80% of deals in 15–20 business days and averaged 18 business days to Phase 1
approval as at March 2026 — but even a simple notifiable deal can't close for ~5 weeks post-
filing. Incompleteness is the difference between a deal closing this quarter and next.

Why this one is interesting: the regime is **eight months old**. Nobody — not the ACCC, not the
firms — has yet accumulated the tacit knowledge of what a complete notification looks like. The
requirement set is being written in practice right now. That's a rare window: usually you're
trying to extract a checklist that's been ossifying for thirty years. Here you could build the
corpus as it forms. It's also directly adjacent to Acquired's own domain.

**EPBC Act environmental approvals.** Information requests explicitly "stop the clock" on
statutory timeframes. The 2026 reforms set a 30-business-day ministerial decision window and a
20-business-day assessment, but left the stop-the-clock provisions untouched — so the statutory
timeframe is a floor, not a ceiling, and incompleteness is how you blow through it.

**NSW probate requisitions** (see doc 09) and **council DA RFIs** are the same mechanic.

### Mechanic B — Strict documentary compliance
*The gate assesses the paperwork, not the underlying reality. The thing being financed is fine;
the documents don't match. Form over substance, by design.*

**Letters of credit — 60–80% first-presentation rejection.** The most extreme number I found.
Banks assess strict documentary compliance rather than the goods, and the most common
discrepancies are timing (expiry, shipment window, presentation period) and poorly prepared
documents. Most are eventually settled — the loss is delay, cost and working capital, not the
payment. Worth checking whether Traydstream and similar already own this space; my expectation
is yes, and if so it's another closed join.

**Security of Payment Act claims (construction).** The harshest penalty structure of anything
here: **missing the deadline permanently extinguishes the right to adjudicate that claim.** A
non-compliant s13 payment claim or a late s14 payment schedule can "instantly neutralise a
party's commercial leverage", and a respondent who misses their window becomes automatically
liable for the full claimed amount. No appeal, no requisition, no second pass — the entitlement
is gone. Both sides face this, which means both sides are potential buyers.

**FDA 510(k) Refuse to Accept.** A 15-day administrative screen before substantive review even
begins, tripped by missing eSTAR sections, unresolvable cross-references, inadequate predicate
identification, missing attachments for the product code. Note what these are: not scientific
deficiencies — clerical ones. And the checklist is published.

### Mechanic C — Evidence-threshold benefit decisions
*A decision about a person, made entirely on documents, where the default answer is no.*

**Disability Support Pension — around 65–70% of applications rejected**, with insufficient
medical evidence the most common reason. There is an advocacy fact sheet devoted specifically to
"insufficient medical evidence" as a rejection ground, and Services Australia publishes a
medical evidence checklist — again, published, still failing.

**TPD and income protection in super** — the 13-point advised/non-advised admittance gap from
doc 09. This is the same mechanic with a commercial buyer attached.

**Prior authorisation (US)** — 82–83% of appealed denials overturned, under 11% appealed.

**Visa refusals** — silent failure plus a possible three-year PIC 4020 ban.

Common structure: the applicant is at their most vulnerable and least capable exactly when the
documentary demand peaks. Ethically this is where the arbitrage is largest and most worth
closing. Commercially it's where **the buyer test fails hardest** — the person who needs it has
no money, and the assessor's incentive runs the other way. Where a contingency-fee intermediary
exists (plaintiff firms in TPD), it's a business. Where one doesn't (DSP), it's a charity.

### Mechanic D — Credential and licence recognition
*A qualified person or firm cannot work until an assessor confirms they are what they say.*

**AHPRA / international medical graduates.** Incomplete applications are held pending further
information, "each delay costing applicants weeks". Primary-source verification means AHPRA and
the AMC cannot accept certified copies — qualifications must be verified by ECFMG directly with
the issuing school and home regulator. Specialist IMGs first face a college comparability
assessment (substantially / partially / not comparable) before the Board decides registration,
and AHPRA is now pushing to redesign the pathway to cut duplication.

Buyer test: **passes unusually well.** Hospitals, health services and locum agencies carry the
cost of an unfilled roster in a documented workforce shortage, and they have budgets. The
doctor's episodic ignorance is subsidised by an employer's continuous pain.

Same mechanic: AFSL/ACL licensing, teacher registration, trades recognition, and any overseas
qualification pathway.

### Mechanic E — The inverted loop, where the assessor causes it
*Same iteration, opposite cause: the gate loses, re-requests and re-reads what it already has.
The applicant is not ignorant — the assessor is disorganised.*

**Corporate and institutional bank onboarding.** Corporate treasury dissatisfaction with KYC
rose from 73% in 2024 to **95% in 2026**; **97%** have considered switching banks over it, up from
83% in 2025; **99%** report lost revenue from onboarding delays. Corporates report being asked for
the same information multiple times for a third consecutive year. Average KYC review: **95 days**
in 2023, up from 84 in 2022. Roughly **one in five** onboarding applications is abandoned over
KYC/AML friction. *(Encompass/Fenergo-sourced — vendors selling KYC software. Directionally
credible given the consistency and the trend, but discount the levels.)*

**Superannuation death benefits** — ASIC found 78% of reviewed files delayed by trustee-
controlled issues, including re-requesting documents already provided.

Why this mechanic matters commercially: it is the **only one where the gatekeeper is unambiguously
the buyer**, because the loop shows up as churn and lost revenue on their own P&L. It's also,
predictably, the most heavily vendored — Fenergo, Encompass and others are already there. The
lesson generalises: *when the assessor pays, the market clears fast.* Which is exactly why the
applicant-side arbitrages persist.

## How to find more without another research pass

Score any candidate on six questions. Four or more yes, and it's worth a look.

1. Does the applicant do this **fewer than five times in a lifetime**, while the assessor does it
   thousands of times a year?
2. Does an incomplete submission cost **calendar time you can't buy back** — a stopped clock, a
   lost queue position, an extinguished right?
3. Is the gate deciding on **documents about reality**, rather than inspecting reality itself?
4. Does the gate **fail silently** — decline rather than ask?
5. Is the applicant's downside **catastrophic** while the assessor's is a marginal handling cost?
6. Is there a **repeat intermediary or affected employer** whose P&L carries the loop?

Question 6 is the one that kills most candidates, and question 1 is the one that creates the
opportunity. Note that a published checklist does **not** disqualify a candidate — see the
letter-of-credit finding.

## Two things this changes for Acquired

**1. Reposition from knowledge to mechanisation.** "We know what the bank wants" invites the
answer "then we'll publish it". "We mechanise completeness against an evolving requirement set"
survives that, and the LC number is the proof: 60–80% failure against a published rulebook.

**2. The buyer-test lesson.** Mechanic E clears fast because the assessor pays. Acquired sits in
the applicant-side category, which is exactly why the opportunity is still open — and exactly
why the bank, not the buyer, is the better commercial counterparty. If the lender's handling
cost on declined files can be put on a slide, that's the pitch.
