# Pricing the loop

20 Aug 2026. Deep-research round 2. The project's first dollar figures. Every estimate below is
labelled, with its assumptions shown, because almost none of these numbers are published — the
whole point of doc 13's measurement gap is that nobody meters the iteration loop.

---

## The finding that says it all

The Commonwealth tried to price one of these loops and **failed**.

The Department of Health and Aged Care commissioned BDO in May 2023 to cost national locum use,
ran it seven months, and published this (*Analysis of Locum use in the Medical Workforce*, March
2025):

> "A major limitation for the project was the **insufficient quantitative information available to
> provide a national view of locum costs.** Similarly, data on locum speciality, setting, location
> and training/experience was unavailable."

And: *"Lack of published data about locums hinders national evidenced-based policy decisions."*

A federal government, with a stakeholder reference group including the AMA, RDAA and every
jurisdictional health department, could not price the consequence of its own workforce gap. **That
is the completeness arbitrage stated by the buyer, in the buyer's own words.**

Its recommendation 5 then names our product category outright: "consideration of an improved,
centralised (national) approach to locum and health services matching such as a **locum vendor
management system, a centralised credentialling system and data collection and reporting system**."

## 1. AHPRA / IMG registration — what 30 days is worth

**Two figures, two different pockets. Never sum them.**

### Method A — locum premium avoided (the health service's ledger): **~$40,800 per position**

Assumptions: consultant locum $1,854.60/day (platform transaction average); agency margin to
hospital 10–30%; travel and accommodation $300–500/day, calibrated against Northern NSW LHD's
$16m of a $148m agency bill (≈11%); permanent alternative a $260,000 package plus 12% super over
230 days = $1,266/day; 30 calendar days ≈ 30 rostered days in a 24/7 service.

Locum cost to service $2,340–$2,911/day, a premium over permanent of $1,074–$1,645/day.
→ **30 days = $32,200 to $49,300. Central $40,800.** *(Estimate.)*

### Method B — billings recovered (the system's ledger): **~$72,500 rural**

From Queensland Health's own published business-case methodology (*Estimating Private General
Practice Earnings*, Info Sheet 04, June 2026) — a government methodology for exactly this
calculation: **5,702 services per GP FTE per year** at an average Medicare benefit of **$58.97**
= $336,247 per GP FTE per year, or **$1,462 per working day**. Bulk-billing incentives sit on top
and are rural-loaded (tripled from 1 July 2026: $26.35 MMM1 rising to $43.15 MMM7), adding
$653–$1,070/day.

Total gross practice revenue per GP FTE: **$2,115/day (MMM1) to $2,532/day (MMM7)**.
→ **30 days forgone = $63,500 to $76,000. Central rural (MMM5) $72,500.** *(Estimate.)*

### Method C — the candidate-loss tail, deliberately not point-estimated

If 30 days is what makes a candidate withdraw or choose another jurisdiction, the loss is not 30
days of cover. It is the placement fee re-incurred (**$45,000–$75,000** on a $300,000 package, at
RCSA-surveyed rates of 15–25%) **plus** another three to six months of locum cover
(**$98,000–$296,000** at Method A rates). **This is the largest term in the equation and its
probability is entirely unpublished.** It belongs in a business case as risk-adjusted upside, not
as a point estimate.

### The scale check that makes the case without arithmetic

**Northern NSW LHD alone spent more than $400,000 per day** on agency nurses and locum doctors in
2022-23 — $148m total, **13% of its entire budget**. Murrumbidgee ~$50m (12% of permanent workforce
budget). Hunter New England $55m in "premium cost of labour". Western NSW's agency nursing went from
$6m to $37m in seven years, up 238% in under three, excluding travel and accommodation.

Western NSW LHD's submission also names our exact structural dynamic:

> "The demand for agency staff increases the agency or locum rate, which in turn further increases
> the attractiveness of locum or agency work… **This self-perpetuating cycle is completely
> unsustainable.**"

Strip 30 days off ten positions in that one district and Method A yields ~$400,000/year against a
$148m agency bill — a 0.27% saving. **Credible precisely because it is small**, and the addressable
population is every local health network in the country.

*(Day rates are platform averages, credibility ~5, and imply an 8-hour day — overtime, on-call and
penalties sit on top. LHD aggregates blend nursing and medical locum spend and cannot be cleanly
split; 2022-23 vintage.)*

## 2. NSW probate — the sharpest single fact in the project

> **Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Regulation (NSW), Schedule 3, Part 1, clause 1(e):
> the fixed costs "include the following legal services — … (e) answering requisitions."**

**Answering requisitions is inside the regulated fixed fee. A NSW probate provider cannot bill for
rework caused by incompleteness. The marginal revenue is zero by force of law.**

This is doc 13's billing-model test, written into a statutory instrument. Every requisition is pure
margin erosion for a scale-fee provider, and no amount of goodwill or client understanding changes
it.

**The regulated scale (first grant, by disclosed asset value):** $250,000 estate → **$2,117**;
$500,000 → **$3,234**; $900,000 → $5,022; $1.5m → $6,300; $2.5m → $7,960; above $10m capped at
$15,500 flat. Court filing fees run nil to $7,399.

**Internal cost of answering one requisition — $241 to $554, mid $398** *(estimate)*. Built from a
paralegal at $60/hour and a solicitor at $103/hour fully loaded, 1.5–3.5 paralegal hours plus
0.3–1.0 solicitor hours to read it, re-contact the executor, obtain the missing evidence, draft and
swear a supplementary affidavit and re-file, plus $120–$240 of file-carry over 4–8 extra weeks.

**As a share of the entire regulated fee: 12.3% on a $500k estate, 18.8% on a $250k estate.** At a
50% gross margin, **one requisition eats a quarter of the file's margin; two eat half.**

And where the provider works on contingency it is worse than the marginal cost suggests. Willed
Law's own page: *"You only pay once probate is granted."* **A requisition defers 100% of the fee.**

### The work-in-progress evidence is the real story

NSW Supreme Court 2024 Provisional Statistics:

| | 2020 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Uncontested probate filings | 26,661 | **30,801** (+15.5%) |
| Pending at 31 December | 2,401 | **11,165** (+365%) |

Disposals fell *below* filings in 2024 (29,936 against 30,801) — the queue is still growing, and
11,165 pending equals **36% of a full year's filings sitting unfinished.** WIP up 365% on volume up
15.5% is the quantitative signature of the loop, and for a fixed-fee provider it is a balance-sheet
problem while an hourly firm simply bills the rework.

### The strategic conclusion: sell prevention, not acceleration

The registry has already taken its own slack out — processing cut 25% from 83 to 60 business days,
and requisition responses now considered within 5–10 working days. **The remaining recoverable time
is almost entirely applicant-side.** Software that stops a requisition issuing captures the whole
4–8 week re-queue; software that speeds the *response* captures very little. Reinforced by the fact
that replies to requisitions are explicitly never expedited.

Market-wide scenarios *(illustration, not measurement)*: at a 20% requisition rate on 30,801
filings, ~$2.45m/year of unbillable rework across NSW; at 30%, $3.68m; at 50%, $6.13m. The only
anchor is the Chief Justice's 2,000+ unanswered stock, which at his stated typical two-month dwell
implies annual flow near 12,000 — a rate around 40%. That rests on an assumed dwell time.

**The highest-value obtainable number in the entire project:** a GIPA request to the NSW Supreme
Court Probate Registry for requisitions issued per application, 2020–2025. No Australian registry
publishes it. It is a public-access request, not an FOI fishing expedition.

## 3. SME acquisition lending — both absences confirmed, and two better numbers instead

**Confirmed independently:** no Australian bank discloses cost-to-assess per business loan
application, credit-analyst hours per file, or a business-banking-segment cost-to-income ratio —
nothing in RBA, APRA or BIS material either. And no Australian source publishes the proportion of
business sales that collapse on finance; AIBB publishes no such survey. *(US SBA-style figures
circulating online — 15–25% of SBA deals losing financing — come from low-quality aggregators under
a different guarantee structure. Do not put them in a business case.)*

Two things to use instead.

### Judo Bank discloses the capacity constraint directly

FY25 (ASX, 19 August 2025): **161 relationship bankers, 66 analysts, 557 staff, 4,621 customers,
$12.5bn gross lending**. Derived: **28.7 customers per relationship banker**, 70 per analyst, and a
**$2.71m average facility — exactly the acquisition-finance ticket band.**

Cost-to-income **52.4%**, guidance below 50% for FY26, and an at-scale target **"approaching 30%"**.
The CEO names the lever: *"working to increase the capacity of our relationship bankers."* The
three-year core replatform is complete, so the integration substrate exists.

**Judo has publicly committed to removing roughly 22 points of cost-to-income. On a ~$183m cost base
that is ~$77m a year. It does not need to be sold the problem.**

Reconstructed cost per originated file *(estimate — Judo does not disclose opex; derived from NIM,
CTI and PBT, cross-checked against impairment guidance to within ~10%)*: $329,000 opex per FTE and
**~$1.14m total attributable cost per relationship banker**, giving **$25,300–$57,000 per originated
file, central ~$38,000, or about 1.4% of a $2.71m facility.** The 40% origination time-share and the
8–18 facilities per year are my assumptions, not Judo's.

### Westpac has already priced the loop, and segmented it for us

Its BizEdge origination platform is *"designed to halve banker processing time and speed up simple
loan decisions to within a day"*, with a 10% reduction achieved in about four months, and **"over
three-quarters of simple deals are now auto-decisioned."** It is also hiring 350 additional bankers
by 2027 — buying the capacity that software substitutes for.

Apply Westpac's own 50% to the Judo-derived $38,000 and the addressable inefficiency is up to
~$19,000 per judgement-intensive file, conservatively ~$9,500 *(estimate)*.

**But the segmentation is the real point, and it is the strongest form of the doc-14 argument.**
Westpac auto-decisions 75%+ of *simple* deals. Business acquisition finance is not simple — target
financials, vendor normalisations, sale agreement, lease assignment, goodwill, often partial vendor
finance. **The completeness problem has already been engineered out of the auto-decisioned tail and
remains fully intact in exactly our segment.**

### And the broker's incentives are aligned with us

Broker commission is 5–12% of sale price (8–10% under $1m, 5–8% over), minimum $15,000–$30,000+, and
it is a **success fee on completion**. A finance failure writes off the broker's entire contingent
economics plus three to nine months of their time. Buyer sunk cost per failed attempt is
**$20,000–$45,000 of non-recoverable professional spend plus 60–120 days** *(low-confidence estimate
built from fee structures, since no Australian survey exists)*.

## 4. FIRB — the number I wanted does not exist, and the replacement is better

**Commercial withdrawals, complete series** (Treasury Quarterly Report, Jan–Mar 2026):

| Year | Approved | Withdrawn | Withdrawn share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-23 | 1,314 | 149 | 10.2% |
| 2023-24 | 1,231 | 246 | **16.7%** |
| 2024-25 | 1,362 | 131 | 8.8% |
| 2025-26 (9 months) | 1,017 | 112 | 9.9% |

**Treasury reports "withdrawn" as one undifferentiated line.** It does not separate
withdraw-and-resubmit from abandonment, and does not report resubmissions at all — **a resubmitted
deal is statistically indistinguishable from a dead one in the only public dataset.** The 2023-24
spike to 16.7% is unexplained; notably Treasury adopted its 30-day performance target on 1 January
2025, immediately after it. Cannot be resolved from public data; FOI is the route.

**The better hook, published quarterly:** Treasury's median processing time for approved commercial
proposals is **35 days** (36 the prior quarter — persistent, not a blip), with **46% decided within
30 days against its own 50% target.** So **54% of applicants exceed the statutory clock and the
regulator is missing its own published target.** That applies to every deal rather than to the
withdrawn tail.

Deal-side cost of a 30–90 day slip *(estimates from market convention, not retrieved term sheets)*:
commitment letters typically run 90–120 days, so a slip routinely passes expiry; extending a
committed acquisition facility conventionally costs 25–75bps. A 90-day extension on a $200m facility
at 50bps ≈ **$1.0m**; on $500m ≈ $2.5m. Paying a top-tier notification fee twice = **$2,343,200**.
Exclusivity of 30–90 days is exhausted, re-exposing the buyer to competition.

## What to do with this

1. **AHPRA has the best buyer case and now has a price.** ~$40,800 per position per 30 days on the
   health service's ledger, against districts already spending $400,000 a day on agency cover.
2. **Probate has the sharpest structural argument.** Requisition answering is inside the regulated
   fee by statute, so the provider's marginal revenue for rework is legally zero, and WIP is up
   365% on volume up 15.5%.
3. **For Acquired, lead with Westpac's segmentation, not with a cost-per-file estimate.** "Three
   quarters of simple deals are auto-decisioned; acquisition finance is what's left" is a stronger
   and more honest claim than any reconstructed number, and it comes from a major bank's own
   platform pitch.
4. **Judo is the warm counterparty.** It has publicly committed to removing ~22 points of
   cost-to-income and named banker capacity as the lever. That is a mutual interest, not a cold pitch.
5. **Two obtainable numbers would change the project:** a GIPA request for the NSW probate
   requisition rate, and one conversation with a bank credit team about analyst hours consumed by a
   declined acquisition file.
